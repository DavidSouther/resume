Question 2: What is the incumbency bonus, by presidential lean?

A positive bonus is points of two party vote share an incumbent of that party gains over an open seat, averaged over lean groups weighted by seats and pooled over years: house DEM +3.3, house REP +4.0, senate DEM +3.1, senate REP +2.4, governor DEM +0.2, governor REP +6.0. The Wald test of equal Democratic and Republican bonuses has p below 0.05 in 2 office and lean groups.

Question 3: Could turnout alone explain each shift?

The file has 2524 races with a turnout multiplier k, with a median of 1.024. Of 2524 races with a statewide comparison, turnout alone is plausible for 1133 and implausible for 1391. A race counts as implausible when the Democratic votes needed far exceed the real change in ballots.

The plausibility columns

The multiplier k assumes every prior voter kept their side and only turnout moved. required_extra_dem_votes is how many Democratic ballots turnout alone would have to add (or remove) to produce the current two party share with Republican votes held fixed. comparison_change is the real change in ballots. On unchanged district lines it is the race's own change in votes cast. For a redrawn race the lines moved, so it is the statewide change in ballots scaled by the race's prior share of the state. slack is 2 percent of the prior two party votes, allowing for ballots that move between the top two candidates and others.

turnout_plausible is true when the required change is no larger than the real change plus slack. It is false when it is larger, and empty when the statewide comparison is not available, for example for 2026 before the Election Lab publishes turnout. A false value means turnout alone is not a believable explanation. It does not show that persuasion happened. flip_share gives the persuasion only reading of the same change.
